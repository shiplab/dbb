Assumptions and limits
  • Buoyancy comes only from the hulls, which are illustrative shapes. Blocks outside a hull, appendages, bulbs and propellers add nothing.
  • The envelope is the box around all blocks, rotated ones included. Overall length, beam and height, and the requirements on them, are read from the envelope, not from the hulls. The hulls are fitted to the blocks below the main deck, so they can be longer or wider than the envelope.
  • The hull drawings and the table of offsets in the PDF come from the same parametric formula as the hydrostatics. They show the illustrative shape the numbers are based on; they are not faired lines.
  • Gross tonnage is an estimate by the 1969 Tonnage Convention from the blocks that count as enclosed spaces. Overlapping blocks count twice. It is not a tonnage certificate.
  • Each block's weight acts at its own centroid. The structure is a percentage of the displacement, spread over the hull volume.
  • Draught is solved with Archimedes' principle: displacement equals the total weight, in a flat sea with one water density.
  • Trim and heel are an exact tilted water plane on the hull shapes, solved so that the volume equals the weight and the centre of buoyancy sits on the vertical through the centre of gravity. All hulls share the plane.
  • The heel needs the vertical centre of gravity, which comes from the block centroids and the structure estimate. A heavy off-centre load on a single hull may have no equilibrium within the range, and then the heel is shown as not found.
  • GM is initial stability only (small angles). It is worked out upright and without trim, at the level draught that carries the weight, from the waterplane of the hulls (BM = I / V). There is no righting-arm curve, large-angle stability, wind, damage or wave effect. The heel above is the equilibrium angle for the loading, not a stability check.
  • Free surface: a block marked Free surface and partly filled is taken as a rectangular tank by its bounding box, which overestimates round or tapered tanks. Only blocks you mark are counted.
  • The basic checks are simple guides (GM at least 0.15 m as in the IMO intact criteria, heel and trim limits as design guides). They are not a stability assessment for class or flag state.
  • The hull checks (blocks outside the hulls, volume below the waterline) use the level draught, also when the trim is shown.
  • Indicative only, for early comparison of arrangements. Not for class or stability documentation.
